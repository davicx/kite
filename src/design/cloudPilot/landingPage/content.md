### Positioning Strip

CloudPilot starts with AWS, but it isn't another AWS dashboard. The goal is an intelligent layer between engineers and everything their software depends on — infrastructure, operational context, organizational knowledge, and eventually the tools around it.

### The Problem

Your systems have plenty of data. They don't understand why anything exists.

Engineers spend too much time piecing together infrastructure data, billing information, documentation, tags, old decisions, and tribal knowledge before they can answer a simple question or safely make a change.

**Your tools know data, not intent**
AWS can tell you CPU utilization and monthly spend. It can't tell you why an instance exists, who depends on it, or whether low utilization is intentional.

**Every answer becomes detective work**
CloudWatch, billing, Terraform, tags, docs, tickets, and conversations all hold part of the answer. Engineers have to reconstruct the story.

**Your organization forgets**
Why was this bucket created? Who owns this instance? Why can't this service be stopped? Important context lives in people's heads and disappears when teams change.

### How CloudPilot Works

Understand your systems. Remember the context. Help when you ask. Act when you approve.

CloudPilot combines live infrastructure information with the knowledge your organization builds over time.

**01 — Understand**
CloudPilot connects to the systems your software runs on and understands what is actually there — resources, configuration, usage, spend, and relationships.

**02 — Remember**
It builds organizational knowledge over time: ownership, purpose, infrastructure relationships, important decisions, and things your team tells it.

**03 — Help**
Ask naturally: Why is this expensive? What is this bucket for? What's running in production? What needs my attention?

**04 — Act**
When you want something changed, CloudPilot can guide you, generate the change, open a pull request, or execute it — with confirmation and safeguards.

### Organizational Knowledge

The more CloudPilot understands your organization, the more useful it becomes.

Infrastructure data tells CloudPilot what exists. Organizational knowledge helps it understand what those resources mean to you.

**What exists — Live infrastructure**
Resources, regions, tags, configuration, usage, cost, and relationships pulled from the systems you actually use.

**What it means — Organizational context**
This bucket stores customer uploads. That EC2 instance is a failover server. This resource belongs to production. This service shouldn't be stopped overnight.

**What you've learned — Team knowledge**
CloudPilot can remember useful context your team has already provided, so the next question doesn't start from zero.

That's the difference between finding an idle resource and understanding one. CloudPilot should know enough context to recognize that something can look wasteful without actually being unnecessary.

### From Answer to Action

Conversation doesn't mean giving AI unrestricted access to production.

CloudPilot separates understanding from execution. It can inspect, explain, and recommend before anything is changed.

**You stay in control**
CloudPilot can start read-only. When a change is requested, it explains what will happen and waits for the appropriate approval before execution.

**Different changes can use different paths**
A simple operational action might be performed directly. Infrastructure changes can become CLI instructions or a pull request you review before anything touches production.

### Built for Two People

The engineer wants less toil. The manager wants a number for the board deck. CloudPilot gives both a reason to open it.

**For the engineer — Stop firefighting cost tickets**
No dashboards to babysit. CloudPilot flags waste as it happens, explains the "why" in the same terms you'd use in a PR review, and drops a fix into your workflow — not a support queue.

**For the manager — A number you can defend**
A running total of savings, verified and timestamped, in language that survives a budget review. No more "we think we saved something" going into the all-hands.

### What's Running Today

Four things CloudPilot does before you've had your coffee

These aren't roadmap slides — this is the MVP, scanning real accounts right now.

**01 — Continuous EC2 scanning**
CloudPilot reads your running instances, volumes, and traffic patterns on a schedule — no manual exports, no CSV wrangling in a spreadsheet.

**02 — AI-generated recommendations**
Every finding comes with a plain-English explanation of the waste, the blast radius of fixing it, and the expected dollar impact — not just a red flag.

**03 — One-click remediation**
Apply a fix in the same interface you found it in. No context-switching into the AWS console to hunt down the resource again.

**04 — Built-in undo**
Every remediation is reversible by default. If a fix turns out to be wrong for your setup, you're one click from exactly where you started.

### Trust Is the Feature

You choose how hands-off CloudPilot gets

Every recommendation can be actioned four different ways — pick per-resource, per-team, or set a default and move on.

**Step-by-step instructions**
Read exactly what to click in the AWS console. You stay fully in control, CloudPilot just does the research.

**PR to your infra repo**
Get a pull request against your Terraform or CDK, reviewed and merged the same way as any other change.

**Auto-approve execution**
For the fixes your team trusts every time — like clearing orphaned snapshots — let CloudPilot just handle it.

**Runnable script**
Grab a standalone script to run in your own CI, on your own schedule, with your own approvals gating it.

Nothing is permanent by accident. Every path — even auto-approve — writes to an audit trail with a one-click undo. Handing an AI access to your AWS account only works if reversing it is as easy as granting it.

### Where This Is Going

The MVP finds waste. The vision is an autopilot for infrastructure decisions.

Scanning EC2 is the first instrument on the panel. Here's the rest of the dashboard we're building toward.

**Next — Multi-service, multi-cloud coverage**
Beyond EC2 into RDS, S3, Lambda, and load balancers — then GCP and Azure, so the same panel works regardless of where a team landed.

**Next — A policy engine, not just a queue of suggestions**
Set standing rules — "auto-approve anything under $50/mo," "always PR, never auto-execute in prod" — and CloudPilot enforces them without you re-deciding every time.

**Later — The manager's report writes itself**
A scheduled, board-ready summary of verified savings, trends, and forecasted spend — delivered to Slack or email, no dashboard login required.

**Later — Cost review, built into code review**
CloudPilot comments directly on infrastructure pull requests before they merge — catching the oversized instance or forgotten dev resource before it ever hits the bill.

**North star — An infrastructure co-pilot lean teams actually trust**
Not a bot that touches your AWS account and hopes for the best — a system that earns the right to act autonomously, one reversible decision at a time, until "autopilot" stops sounding like a leap of faith.

### Why Access Is Earned

That's why CloudPilot starts read-only. See the recommendations, see the dollar impact, see exactly what would change — before granting anything more. Trust builds one correct, undoable action at a time, not on day one.

### Organizational Knowledge Vision

CloudPilot doesn't just understand your cloud. It understands your organization.

Over time, CloudPilot learns what your infrastructure is for, who owns it, what projects depend on it, what your team is working on, and what has happened before.

That changes CloudPilot from "AI that can operate AWS" into "AI that understands what's going on across your technology organization."

**What "knows your organization" means:**
- Your infrastructure — what's running, what it costs, what depends on what, what's healthy, and what's being wasted.
- Your projects — what your team is building, deploying, fixing, and planning.
- Your people — who owns a service, who worked on something recently, and who might know about a problem.
- Your conversations — important context from tools like Slack and email, when connected and authorized.
- Your work — tickets, incidents, tasks, deployments, pull requests, and decisions from tools such as Jira.
- Your history — what changed, why it changed, what CloudPilot did, and what happened afterward.

CloudPilot connects the dots between all of them.

**Example question:** "Why is our AWS bill higher this month?"

Basic answer: EC2 spending increased by $1,240.

CloudPilot long-term answer: EC2 spending increased after the team launched the new image-processing service two weeks ago. The additional servers belong to the Kite project. Based on the Jira launch ticket and recent activity, three were created for load testing but are still running. Sarah's team owns them. Turning those three off could save approximately $620/month.

### Remediation Types (Public Framing)

You decide how CloudPilot helps.

**Instructions — Show me how.**
CloudPilot explains exactly what needs to be changed and walks your team through it.

**CLI — Give me the command.**
For technical teams that want a quick, ready-to-run solution.

**Pull Request — Prepare the change for my team.**
CloudPilot creates the infrastructure change so your team can review and approve it through their normal process.

**Automatic — Take care of it for me.**
For approved actions, CloudPilot can safely make the change directly.
